# Style rules for the Zimlet documentation

Section 9 of the writing rules in [write-doc.md](../write-doc.md). Read it in full together with that file. These rules apply with section 2. The section numbers are the same as in `write-doc.md`.

## 9. Style

These rules decide how a page reads.

- Lead with the outcome. Open a page, a section and a step with what the reader gets or does, then give the detail.
- Do not talk about the page. Do not write "This page gives you…", "This section explains…" or "In this example…". Say the thing itself.
- Do not restate a diagram or an example in prose. The text adds what the picture or code cannot show, such as the reason, the condition or the next step. No "In words:" paragraph.
- Do not name internal tools, repositories, source files or code paths. Say what the reader sees or does.
- Keep sentences short, about 25 words at most, with one idea each. Split a sentence that stacks clauses. Avoid chains of "which" and "that"; use two sentences.
- Use at most one admonition per section. Use none when a plain sentence is enough. An admonition holds one point.
- Keep a concept page to about one screen of text. Put a mechanism, an option list or an edge case on the page where the reader needs it, and link to it.
- Put the most common case first. Mention an exception only when the reader can hit it at this point.
- Use the same words for the same thing throughout, and the same order for parallel items.
- Use a professional, neutral tone in titles, headings and text: complete, courteous sentences, no slang, no blunt or dismissive phrasing, no greetings or filler such as "Welcome" or "Let's get started", and no titles that are bare nouns. Name a page for what it is (`Zimlet developer guide`, not `Zimlet`).
- Do not break words with hyphens. Never type a hyphen, soft hyphen (`&shy;`) or line break inside a word to fit a line. The site turns off automatic hyphenation (`documentation/supplemental-ui/css/site-extra.css`), so a word always stays whole. Hyphens belong only in real compound words, such as `self-signed`.
